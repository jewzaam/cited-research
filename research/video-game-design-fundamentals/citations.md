## Tier rubric

- **Tier 1:** Peer-reviewed paper, court ruling, government/institutional report, original primary source.
- **Tier 2:** Established trade press with named author and editorial standards (Game Developer, GDC Vault, GamesIndustry.biz, major newspaper), industry analytics firm with published methodology, manufacturer/platform documentation.
- **Tier 3:** Practitioner blog, industry consultancy, well-known designer/marketer essay.
- **Tier 4:** Forum, Reddit, social media, aggregator without primary methodology.

## Core design fundamentals

[1] **Hunicke, R., LeBlanc, M., & Zubek, R. (2004).** "MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research." *Proceedings of the AAAI Workshop on Challenges in Game AI*. https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf — **Tier 1.** Source paper for the MDA framework. Eight aesthetic categories: Sensation, Fantasy, Narrative, Challenge, Fellowship, Discovery, Expression, Submission. Designer-side runs Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics; player-side experiences Aesthetics ← Dynamics ← Mechanics. Developed at GDC workshops 2001–2004.

[2] **Sid Meier (GDC 2012), via Game Developer reporting.** "GDC 2012: Sid Meier On How To See Games As Sets Of 'Interesting Decisions.'" https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions — **Tier 2.** Meier's 1989 formulation "a game is a series of interesting decisions" elaborated; insignificant decisions take the same cognitive load as interesting ones but produce no satisfaction; Soren Johnson's complementary formula fun = (meaningful decisions) / (time played).

[3] **Swink, S. (2008).** *Game Feel: A Game Designer's Guide to Virtual Sensation.* Morgan Kaufmann / Routledge. ISBN 9780123743282. https://www.routledge.com/Game-Feel-A-Game-Designers-Guide-to-Virtual-Sensation/Swink/p/book/9780123743282 — **Tier 2.** Defines game feel as "realtime control of virtual objects in a simulated space, with interactions emphasised by polish." Internet Archive copy: https://archive.org/details/game-feel.

[4] **Pichlmair, M., & Johansen, M. (2020).** "Designing Game Feel: A Survey." *arXiv:2011.09201*. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09201 — **Tier 1.** Academic survey acknowledging that game feel as a generalizable concept does not extend cleanly beyond action/platformer contexts; Swink's framework requires real-time control and spatial simulation.

[5] **Koster, R. (2004, 2nd ed. 2013).** *A Theory of Fun for Game Design.* O'Reilly. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/theory-of-fun/9781449363208/ — **Tier 2.** "Fun = learning"; pattern mastery as the underlying engagement mechanism; chunking as cognitive process. Press page: https://www.theoryoffun.com/press.shtml.

[6] **Schell, J. (3rd ed. 2019).** *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses.* Routledge. https://www.routledge.com/The-Art-of-Game-Design-A-Book-of-Lenses-Third-Edition/Schell/p/book/9781138632059 — **Tier 2.** 100-lens framework for design diagnosis; Elemental Tetrad (Mechanics, Story, Aesthetics, Technology). Won Game Developer Magazine Front Line Award (1st ed., 2008).

[7] **Ismail, R. (2022).** "Prototypes and the Vertical Slice." *Levelling The Playing Field.* https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/prototypes-and-vertical-slice/ — **Tier 2.** Practitioner framework: prototype = "should you make this?" (disposable, fast, cheap); vertical slice = "can you make this?" (production quality, near-shipping). Quoted: "Most indies mix up the purpose of the Prototype and the Vertical Slice and lose out on a lot of time and money."

[8] **Francis, T. (2018, GDC), via Kris Graft / Game Developer.** "Scope creep: A useful, treacherous tool, says Heat Signature dev." https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scope-creep-a-useful-treacherous-tool-says-i-heat-signature-i-dev — **Tier 2.** Direct quote from a successful solo developer: "Scope creep is a bad, dirty term, yet it's also been my fundamental development technique." Heat Signature shipped roughly two years late. Four rules: pick an idea quick to prototype; prototype the important parts; decide which is the core; creep as far as you like in that direction.

[9] **Jonasson, M., & Purho, P. (GDC Europe 2012).** "Juice It or Lose It." GDC Vault: https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose. Internet Archive / YouTube fallback: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg — **Tier 2.** Live demonstration that layering particles, screenshake, sound, and animation transforms a bare Pong clone — establishes the generalizable principle that perceived game feel is largely additive polish.

[10] **Nijman, J. W. (Vlambeer) (INDIGO 2013).** "The Art of Screenshake." https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U; archive: https://archive.org/details/the-art-of-screenshake — **Tier 3.** Practitioner talk presenting 30 concrete techniques for game feel; introduces the "sleep" frame-pause technique (~0.2s on hit) as nearly invisible but perceptually load-bearing.

## Player psychology and engagement

[11] **Ryan, R. M., Rigby, C. S., & Przybylski, A. (2006).** "The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach." *Motivation and Emotion*, 30, 344–360. https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf (also Springer DOI 10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8, paywalled) — **Tier 1.** Foundational SDT-applied-to-games paper. Studies 1–3 (lab) found in-game autonomy and competence predict enjoyment and pre/post well-being changes. Study 4 (multiplayer survey) confirmed all three needs — autonomy, competence, relatedness — independently predict enjoyment and intent to continue play.

[12] **Tyack, A., & Mekler, E. D. (2024).** "Self-Determination Theory and HCI Games Research: Unfulfilled Promises and Unquestioned Paradigms." *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*. arXiv preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12639 — **Tier 1.** Reviewed N=259 HCI games papers using SDT. 54.83% used SDT only descriptively; only 6 papers across the entire corpus contested any SDT tenet; PENS scale validation commitments largely unfulfilled. Conclusion: SDT functions as an unquestioned paradigm in HCI games research.

[13] **Karhulahti, V.-M. (2024).** "Vitality Structures in 'Addictive' Game Design." *Open Research Europe.* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162526/ — **Tier 1.** Argues "addictive" lacks construct validity as a design descriptor — "it is unclear whether 'addictive behaviour' should equally apply to someone being distracted by checking their smartphone regularly … and another person playing a massive multiplayer online game for decades." Proposes vitality structures (CLIMB, FINAL STRETCH, ALERT) with hypothesized links to ADHD/ASD (CLIMB), OCD (FINAL STRETCH), anxiety (ALERT). Methodology: 110 Finnish treatment-seekers + author's reflective phenomenology. Author explicitly frames as "evidence-based hypotheses" requiring validation.

[14] **Niknejad, A., Mildner, T., Zargham, N., Putze, F., & Malaka, R. (2024).** "Level Up or Game Over: Exploring How Dark Patterns Shape Mobile Games." *ACM MUM 2024.* https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1 — **Tier 1.** N=1,496 mobile games analyzed via the community database darkpatterns.games. 85,000+ dark pattern instances identified. Only 10.76% of games had zero reported dark patterns. 96.8% of "dark" games used F2P vs. 53% of "healthy" games; 93.6% IAP in dark vs. 54% in healthy; 52.4% ads in dark vs. 37.3% in healthy. Four pattern categories: Temporal, Monetary, Social, Psychological.

[15] **Bartle, R. (1996).** "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs." Wikipedia summary with primary citations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartle_taxonomy_of_player_types — **Tier 2.** Four player types from MUD observation: Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers. Original sample was MUD players in 1996, a non-representative sample of current gaming.

[16] **Yee, N. / Quantic Foundry.** "Gamer Motivation Model." https://quanticfoundry.com/gamer-motivation-model/ — **Tier 2.** Factor-analytic model derived from 140,000+ gamers (later expanded to 1.75M+). 12 motivations in 6 clusters: Action, Social, Mastery, Achievement, Creativity, Immersion. Empirically displaced Bartle's typology in research circles, though Bartle remains widely taught. Caveat: Quantic Foundry is a commercial firm; model is proprietary, no peer-reviewed independent replication.

[17] **GameAnalytics (2025).** *Mobile Gaming Benchmarks 2025*, summarized via GameDev Reports. https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks — **Tier 2.** 11,600 gaming applications, 9 regions, 2 platforms, 16 genres, 1.48B MAU. Median D1 ~15%, median D7 3.42–3.94%, median D28 ~0.85% across the full population. Top-25% D1 26.48–27.69%, D7 7–8%, D28 ~3%+. iOS top-25% D1 31–33% vs. Android 25–27%. Both medians declined from 2023 levels.

[18] **Mistplay / AppsFlyer Q3 2022 data.** "The big list of mobile game retention benchmarks." https://business.mistplay.com/resources/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks — **Tier 2.** Match games: D1 32.65%, D7 13.98%, D30 7.15%. Hyper-casual: D1 29.31%, D7 5.90%, D30 1.38% — a ~5x D30 spread across genres.

[19] **Solsten (2025).** "The True Drivers Of D1, D7, And D30 Retention In Gaming." https://solsten.io/blog/d1-d7-d30-retention-in-gaming — **Tier 3.** Industry analyst framing: D1 = "finding the fun"; D7 = "feeling progression and social hooks"; D30 = "sunk cost and commitment." Practitioner consensus that D1 acts as a ceiling for downstream retention.

[20] **Abuhamdeh, S. (2020).** "Investigating the 'Flow' Experience: Key Conceptual and Operational Issues." *Frontiers in Psychology.* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7033418/ — **Tier 1.** Reviewed 42 flow studies and identified 24 distinct operationalizations. 17/42 studies excluded enjoyment despite Csikszentmihalyi defining it as central. The flow construct has no stable empirical identity in games research.

[21] **Hodent, C. (former Epic UX Director, PhD).** "Ethics in the Videogame Industry." https://celiahodent.com/ethics-in-the-videogame-industry/ — **Tier 3.** Distinguishes engagement, excessive gaming, problematic gaming, and addictive gaming (DSM-5 IGD). Estimates only 0.3–1% of the global player population qualifies for gaming disorder. Caveat: practitioner essay, not peer-reviewed; specific dopamine comparisons cited in the post require primary verification not located in this run.

## Production process and scope management

[22] **Weersing, J. (2024).** "The Last Humble Bee Postmortem: Staying sane in solo development." Game Developer. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-last-humble-bee-postmortem-staying-sane-in-solo-development — **Tier 2.** Solo developer postmortem. **Original estimate: 9 months. Actual: 27 months (3x overrun).** Daily commitment 10 minutes to 2 hours. Engine: GameMaker (GML). Game launched November 21, 2024 at $4.99. References Scott Cawthon's ~70 games before FNAF as a "keep going" anchor.

[23] **Mirabello, J. (2014).** "How long does it take to make an indie game?" [26] **Stack Overflow Blog (2021), feature on Tarn Adams.** "700,000 lines of code, 20 years, and one developer: How Dwarf Fortress is built." https://stackoverflow.blog/2021/12/31/700000-lines-of-code-20-years-and-one-developer-how-dwarf-fortress-is-built/ — **Tier 2.** Adams develops Dwarf Fortress since 2002 (full-time since 2006). ~711,000 lines of code, solo. [27] **GamesRadar (2024), via Iain Harris.** "Caves of Qud was nine years in Steam Early Access." https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/legendary-roguelike-rpgs-9-years-in-steam-early-access-was-possible-as-devs-wanted-a-positive-lifestyle-and-great-game-rather-than-maximum-profit/ — **Tier 2.** Freehold Games (Jason Grinblat, Brian Bucklew) — 2-person team. **9 years in Steam Early Access** (2015 entry, December 2024 1.0 exit). ## Disciplines and AI-augmented workflows

[29] **METR (Becker, J., Rush, N., Barnes, E., & Rein, D.) (2025).** "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity." https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/. Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089 — **Tier 1.** Randomized controlled trial. **n=16 experienced open-source developers, 246 issues, ~2 hours each.** Developers using Cursor Pro + Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet **took 19% longer** than without AI. Pre-study prediction: 24% speedup; post-study self-perception: 20% speedup — actual: 19% slowdown. Compensation $150/hr. Caveat (study itself notes): mature OSS codebases, experienced developers, Cursor familiarity averaged ~50 hours.

[30] **Alharthi, S. A. (University of Jeddah) (2025).** "Generative AI in Game Design." *Journal of Intelligence* (MDPI). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12193870/ — **Tier 1.** Survey n=42 game design professionals, 9 interviews. 90.5% use AI for ideation/brainstorming; 83.3% for asset creation; 73.8% for programming assistance; 75%+ agree AI accelerates task completion. **60%+ worried about reduced originality.** ChatGPT used by 90.5%, Midjourney 76.2%, Copilot 69.0%. Quote: "AI recognizes patterns and predicts likely outputs, but it doesn't grasp the cascading effects of a single tweak on balance, readability, or emotional tone." Caveat: small N, single author, MDPI open-access journal.

[31] **Stack Overflow (2025).** "2025 Developer Survey: AI section." https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/ (full data: https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai/) — **Tier 2.** **84% use or plan to use AI tools (up from 76% in 2024). 46% distrust AI accuracy (up from 31% in 2024).** 45% cite debugging AI-generated code as time-consuming; 75.3% wouldn't trust AI to handle most coding. 61.7% have ethical/security concerns about AI code. 64% don't view AI as a job threat. Claude Code adoption 10% among surveyed developers.

[32] **Game Developers Conference / Omdia (2025).** "GDC 2025 State of the Game Industry: devs weigh in on layoffs, AI, and more." https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more/ — **Tier 2.** **n=3,000+ developers, ±2% MoE.** **36% personally use generative AI tools. 30% believe gen AI negatively impacts the industry (up 12 pts year-over-year).** Adoption by role: Business/Finance 51%, Production/Leadership 41%, Community/Marketing 39%. **56% of respondents have self-funded a game.**

[33] **Google Cloud / Harris Poll (Aug 2025).** "90% of Games Developers Already Using AI in Workflows." https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-08-18-90-of-Games-Developers-Already-Using-AI-in-Workflows-According-to-New-Google-Cloud-Research — **Tier 2.** n=615, 5 countries (US, South Korea, Finland, Norway, Sweden). 90% report using AI in workflows; 95% say it reduces repetitive tasks; 47% use AI for playtesting/balancing; 44% for code/scripting; 45% for localization. 63% concerned about data ownership. Caveat: commissioned by Google Cloud; "AI in workflows" definition broad enough that the figure is not directly comparable to GDC's 36% personal use.

[34] **Game Developer (2025).** "Developers still aren't warming up to generative AI." https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/developers-still-aren-t-warming-up-to-generative-ai — **Tier 2.** Sentiment shift 2024→2025: 30% believe gen AI harms industry (up from 18%); positive view dropped from 21% to 13%. **52%** of game industry professionals believe AI has a negative industry impact per the GDC 2026 follow-up survey.

[35] **Hadan, H., et al. (2024).** "F2P transitions and exploitation patterns." CHI PLAY 2024. https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22901v1 — **Tier 1.** 346 Reddit posts analyzed. 92% of players reported battle pass as "worst among F2P titles." Players distinguish core engagement from manipulation; auxiliary mechanics drove the negative perception.

[36] **Generative AI in Game Development: A Qualitative Research Synthesis (Sept 2025).** arXiv. https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11898v1 — **Tier 1.** Synthesis finding: unedited AI outputs "rarely suitable for direct inclusion." 3D asset gap is persistent. Code generation limited to "relatively simple tasks." Ideation acceleration does not reduce downstream refinement burden.

[37] **Wikipedia.** "2024–2025 SAG-AFTRA video game strike." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike — **Tier 2.** Strike July 26, 2024 – July 9, 2025 (11 months 1 week). Ratified at **95.04%** approval. Settlement established consent and disclosure for AI digital replicas; annual wage bumps over 3 years; performers retain ability to suspend consent during future strikes. Affected ~2,600 voice actors and motion-capture artists.

[38] **Video Games Chronicle (2026).** "Valve has significantly rewritten Steam's rules for how developers must disclose AI use." https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use/ — **Tier 2.** January 2026 policy rewrite: efficiency AI tools now exempt from disclosure; player-facing AI assets still require disclosure. Approximately 8,000 games disclosed AI use by mid-2025 (one in five new releases).

[39] **NYU Journal of Intellectual Property & Entertainment Law (2024).** "Andersen v. Stability AI: The Landmark Case Unpacking the Copyright Risks of AI Image Generators." https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/andersen-v-stability-ai-the-landmark-case-unpacking-the-copyright-risks-of-ai-image-generators/ — **Tier 1.** August 2024 ruling allowed all copyright claims to proceed. [40] **Aftermath (2025).** "AI Video Game Development." https://aftermath.site/ai-video-game-development-art-vibe-coding-midjourney/ — **Tier 2.** Investigative reporting. Senior game designer described AI as "an affront to my expertise." [41] **Frontier Developments (2025), via Game Developer.** "Frontier yanks generative AI portraits from Jurassic World Evolution 3 after fans revolt." https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/frontier-yanks-generative-ai-portraits-from-jurassic-world-evolution-3-after-fans-revolt — **Tier 2.** Documented case: AI scientist portraits removed pre-launch after wishlist deletions and player protest. ## Commercial success — indie and mobile

[42] **Ziva (2026).** "Steam Indie Game Revenue: How Much Do Indie Games Really Make?" https://ziva.sh/blogs/indie-game-revenue — **Tier 2** (synthesis of Tier 1–2 sources). ## Failure modes

[73] **MobileSyrup / SuperScale via Atomik Research (Nov 2023).** "Mobile games failure rate report." https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/11/23/mobile-games-failure-rate-report/ — **Tier 2.** **n=500 UK/US mobile developers. 83% of mobile games fail to survive beyond 3 years. 43% are cancelled before launch. 76% reach peak revenue in year one; only 4% peak in year two.** Caveat: SuperScale is a live-ops vendor — has commercial interest in framing post-launch failure as recoverable.

[74] **How To Market A Game / Chris Zukowski (Nov 2022).** "The median indie game does not earn a whole lot." https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/11/28/the-median-indie-game-does-not-earn-a-whole-lot/ — **Tier 2.** 2019 Steam cohort: median lifetime gross $1,136 (~$795 net after Valve cut). 68th percentile under $10,000. Methodology relies on SteamSpy estimates with known undercounting.

[75] **Bruno, D.** "Failure postmortems collection." https://danbruno.net/notes/games/failure-postmortems/ — **Tier 2.** Curated compilation of indie failure postmortems with sales numbers. Examples include *Super Win the Game* (~900 first month), *Airscape* (~150 lifetime), *Trials of Azra* (~200 first month).

[76] **Game Developer (2018), Strelkov, A.** "How I wasted $4K and half a year of my life to develop a game that earned only $30." https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-i-wasted-4k-and-half-a-year-of-my-life-to-develop-a-game-that-earned-only-30 — **Tier 2.** *Drunk Shotgun* mobile postmortem. **$4,006 development cost; $35.57 revenue. CPI $0.41; LTV $0.02; CAC $120.** 55 work days while employed as CTO. Facebook/Instagram rejected ads for "guns, violence, blood and alcohol references."

[77] **Kotaku (2025).** "Steam: 19,000 new games, the majority unplayed." https://kotaku.com/steam-19-000-new-games-limited-pc-valve-unplayed-80-1851738322 — **Tier 2.** SteamDB: 79% (~14,951) of 2024 Steam releases classified "Limited" — failed to hit undisclosed sales/engagement thresholds. Worsened from 66% in 2020 and 72% in 2022. Approximately 50% of 2025 Steam releases earned fewer than 10 reviews; ~10% had zero reviews.

[78] **Haddad, T. (2023).** "The Video Game Industry Has a Survivorship Bias Problem." Medium. https://medium.com/@tylerehaddad/the-video-game-industry-has-a-survivorship-bias-problem-e313466ab907 — **Tier 3.** Argument: failed devs are silent (no audience, no reputational incentive); success narratives hide invisible advantages (savings, family support, prior industry experience); advice generalizes from outliers.

[79] **Khabibrakhmanov, I. (Innova Systems, GDC 2014).** "Everybody Lies: Survivorship Bias." https://gdcvault.com/play/1020792/Everybody-Lies-Survivorship — **Tier 2.** GDC presentation. Industry data collection skews toward satisfied users; dissatisfied users disengage silently. Frameworks built only on visible success produce flawed strategic guidance.

[80] **Kilduff-Taylor, P. (2018).** "The 10 secrets to indie game success (and why they do not exist)." Game Developer. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-10-secrets-to-indie-game-success-and-why-they-do-not-exist- — **Tier 2.** Practitioner argument: there are no transferable success patterns, only "luck and effort." Marketing tactics that worked briefly become obsolete. Even experienced studios (Mojang, Boss Key) cannot reliably replicate their own successes.

[81] **Cliff Harris / Positech Games (2017).** "Your indie game will flop and you will lose money." https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2017/06/23/your-indie-game-will-flop-and-you-will-lose-money/ — **Tier 2.** Experienced solo dev. "The default position for an indie game developer is pretty much poverty." Even 36 years of coding and a profitable back catalog don't reliably generate press for a new release.

[82] **Cliff Harris / Positech Games (2021).** "Common Mistakes by Indie Game Developers." https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/10/14/common-mistakes-by-indie-game-developers/ — **Tier 2.** Specific failure patterns: choosing oversaturated genres without genre expertise; engine/tool switching mid-project; treating marketing as optional; insufficient post-launch support (recommends 12 months / 40+ patches).

[83] **Game World Observer (Apr 2023), via Florent Maurin (The Pixel Hunt).** "The Wreck post-launch case." https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/04/19/game-discovery-the-wreck-launch-indiedev — **Tier 2.** *The Wreck:* Rock Paper Shotgun "Bestest Best," ~20,000 Steam wishlists, **~1,000 sold at launch.** "Press coverage doesn't magically convert people."
